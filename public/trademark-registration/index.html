The most common starting point

Brand Name (Word Mark)

A word mark protects the name itself, in any font, style or colour. It is filed with the Trade Marks Registry under the Trade Marks Act, 1999, in the class or classes that match your goods or services.

Who may consider this?

  • May suit businesses that want to protect their brand, product or service name, including names used on websites, packaging and online marketplaces.
  • The applicant can be an individual, proprietor, partnership firm, LLP or company. A separate application is generally needed for each class.

Documents & details to prepare

  • Brand name exactly as you want it registered
  • Applicant's name, address and type (individual, firm, LLP or company)
  • PAN and identity/address proof of the applicant or authorised signatory
  • List of goods or services the brand is or will be used for
  • Date of first use, if the brand is already in use
  • User affidavit with proof of use, if you are claiming prior use
  • MSME (Udyam) or Startup India certificate, if eligible for the lower government fee
  • Signed authorisation (Form TM-48) for filing through an agent

Initial checklist for a typical India-based applicant. Additional documents may apply depending on the applicant type, the mark and current Trade Marks Registry requirements. Final requirements should be confirmed by the professional handling the filing.

For brands with a distinctive design

Logo (Device Mark)

A device mark protects a logo or stylised design as it appears. If your logo includes the brand name, a separate word-mark application can also be considered for broader protection.

Who may consider this?

  • May suit businesses that already have a final logo used on signage, packaging, social media or product labels.
  • The logo should be final before filing; changing the design later usually needs a fresh application.

Documents & details to prepare

  • Clear image (JPG) of the final logo
  • Applicant's name, address and type (individual, firm, LLP or company)
  • PAN and identity/address proof of the applicant or authorised signatory
  • List of goods or services the logo is or will be used for
  • Date of first use and proof of use, if the logo is already in use
  • MSME (Udyam) or Startup India certificate, if eligible for the lower government fee
  • Signed authorisation (Form TM-48) for filing through an agent

Initial checklist for a typical India-based applicant. Additional documents may apply depending on the applicant type, the mark and current Trade Marks Registry requirements. Final requirements should be confirmed by the professional handling the filing.

Recommended before filing

Check Before You File (Trademark Search)

A trademark search checks the Registry's records for identical or similar marks in the relevant classes. It helps you judge the risk of objection or opposition before you invest in filing.

Who may consider this?

  • May suit anyone choosing a new brand name, or planning to file and wanting to reduce the chance of objections.
  • A search is indicative: it does not guarantee registration, and the Registry's examination decides the application.

Documents & details to prepare

  • Brand name (and logo, if any) you want to check
  • Short description of your products or services
  • Classes you think apply, if known
  • Markets or states where you sell or plan to sell

Initial checklist for a typical India-based applicant. Additional documents may apply depending on the applicant type, the mark and current Trade Marks Registry requirements. Final requirements should be confirmed by the professional handling the filing.

For applications already filed

Objection or Opposition Reply

After filing, the Registry may raise objections in an examination report, or a third party may file an opposition. A reply usually has to be filed within the time limit given in the notice, or the application may be treated as abandoned.

Who may consider this?

  • May suit applicants whose trademark status shows Objected or Opposed, or who have a hearing scheduled.
  • Time limits apply: a reply to an examination report is generally due within 30 days of receiving it. Check the date on your notice.

Documents & details to prepare

  • Trademark application number
  • Copy of the examination report or opposition notice
  • Proof of use (invoices, advertisements, website or packaging) with dates
  • User affidavit, if prior use is being claimed
  • Signed authorisation (Form TM-48) if a new agent will handle it

Initial checklist for a typical India-based applicant. Additional documents may apply depending on the applicant type, the mark and current Trade Marks Registry requirements. Final requirements should be confirmed by the professional handling the filing.
